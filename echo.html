Magazine / Graphic Design — 2008 · Private Client

Echo

The first edition of Echo, a lifestyle magazine the studio designed and announced in August 2008 for that September: a photographic cover with a stencil masthead and a red contents column, a blackletter feature spread of nine numbered rules round a blue figure on a yellow sunburst, and a pink relationships regular set solid in coral.

The post is an announcement rather than a case study, and it is two sentences long: Revival had designed the first edition of the Echo lifestyle magazine, it was due “sometime in September this year”, and “these are some of the pages from the magazine”. Nothing else about the commission is stated — no publisher, editor, print run or frequency — so the listing carries a Private Client and the bare Ghana the studio works from, and this post is the only place the magazine appears in the archive at all.

The date does not rest on the blog. The cover prints its own: a red lozenge reading MAIDEN ISSUE beside SEPTEMBER 2008, which is what an August announcement of a September issue predicts. The cover also carries the issue’s contents column, and that is how more of the magazine is known than was ever shown — an up-close interview with Chris Attoh at page 18, the love is in the air regular at page 27, and a feature on Prof Andam at page 11. The regular is listed at 27 on the cover while the page published from it carries the number 29.

The three pages published are a deliberate range rather than a set of lookalikes. The cover is photographic, built on diagonals and a distressed stencil masthead. Page 28 is typographic and illustrated: a blackletter headline, a flat blue figure on a yellow sunburst, nine numbered rules hung around it, and a strip of four comic panels at the foot. Page 29 is a text page — a pink regular carried entirely in coral body copy, opening on the question of whether a pocket is empty, with a scrollwork-and-hearts margin down the right edge. Between them they cover the three jobs a magazine designer actually has: a cover that sells the issue, a feature that carries an idea, and a page that carries words.

Only these three pages were ever released. The studio went on to post other graphic-design work through 2008 and 2009, but no later post shows the finished issue or mentions Echo again, so the listing keeps the announcement’s own framing — some of the pages — rather than implying a full table of contents was published. Nor is any software claimed: unlike most of the studio’s 2008 posts, this one carries no labels at all, so the page names no tools.

The entry has been revised since — its feed record shows an update in October 2012 — but nothing about the work is dated from that. The announcement is August 2008 and the cover says September 2008, and those two agree.

The source post is on blogspot — Coming soon this year!!!, 21 August 2008.
