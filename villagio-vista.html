Photography / Architectural Photography — 2015 · RDVS Studios

Villagio Vista

Villagio Vista is a 2015 in-house photography experiment: nine plates drawn from just three exposures of a pixel-clad residential development, one of them a thirty-second night frame cropped six different ways.

The gallery is titled VILLAGIO VISTA - PHOTOGRAPHY EXPERIMENT and was published on 9 July 2015 from the studio’s RDVS. DESIGN Behance account, where it carries an Attribution, Non-commercial, No Derivatives licence. Nine modules were published and all nine are plated here. Four days after Airport City, and in the same run that also produced LA MAISON - PHOTOGRAPHY, WEST HILLS MALL - PHOTOGRAPHY and LINES - EXPERIMENTAL PHOTOGRAPHY, this gallery is the fourth of five photography listings the studio put up in six weeks. Unlike Airport City it reached us with no description and no credits line, so this page carries no Team row and quotes nothing the studio did not write about it; what it does carry is what the files themselves record.

That record is unusually complete, because the studio exported the plates out of Photoshop with their metadata intact. Every frame came off the same Canon EOS 60D, body serial 2571412391, on the kit EF-S18-55mm f/3.5-5.6 IS II at f/13 or wider, and the nine plates resolve to three shutter releases rather than nine. Plates one and two are one daylight capture, IMG_1786.CR2, made at 14:49:25 on 7 July 2015 at 1/200 s, f/13, ISO 100. Plate three is a second daylight capture, IMG_1795.CR2, at 15:30:37, 1/100 s, f/11, ISO 100. Plates four to nine are all one night exposure, IMG_2012.CR2, at 19:37:25 that same evening: a thirty-second frame at f/7.1 and ISO 160 with the exposure programme recorded as manual. Six plates, one press of the shutter, six different crops of it.

What was photographed is a residential development whose towers are clad in an irregular mosaic of coloured rectangular panels, broken by stacks of plain white recessed balconies. The daylight pair holds two towers and the low block joining them against a flat pale sky, and the two plates are one crop published twice over, which the files support rather than prove: the same 1000 by 582 output, the same original RAW, the same creation timestamp to the hundredth of a second. Plate two is full colour, the near tower yellow and cream and the far tower and podium green, while plate one is that frame desaturated to grey, so the cladding becomes a study in three values and the red RDVSIMAGING badge is the only thing left in colour. A small white square tag sits beside the badge on every plate except that first one, which is also the only file that records no live text layers. Plate three pushes in on the yellow tower where an upper volume oversails the floors below, the checkered cladding wrapping over the soffit of the overhang so the pattern runs diagonally across its underside.

The night run is where the experiment is. A thirty-second exposure turns the site into a set of light sources: the towers lit flat white from below, a handful of windows glowing warm orange, red obstruction lights burning at the roof corners, stars holding still as points in a deep blue sky, and traffic reduced to a horizontal smear of white and red that crosses the frame at the waist of the tall tower and streaks the road along the bottom. The six crops of that one exposure are sequenced by how much they let in. Heights at a constant 1000 pixels of width run 543, 769, 1167, 2222, 1896 and 1512, so the listing opens tight on the lit facade, takes the crown of the red tower with its planted and floodlit roof, pulls back across two towers, opens out to the tallest plate which holds the whole scene including the road, and then steps back down through two shorter crops, the last of them the broadest view in the set and the only one to take in the third tower and the green block below it.

The dates the files carry do not agree with the date the gallery claims, and the disagreement is informative. Last-save stamps run plate three at 12:04 on 9 July, plates four to six at 22:45 to 22:48 that night, plates seven to nine in the small hours of 10 July, and then plates one and two at 13:49 and 20:38 on 18 July — nine days after the publish date Behance shows, which is a way of saying that the two daylight plates opening the gallery were the last things the studio touched. The upload stamps inside the module filenames put all nine files on the CDN on 2 November 2015 in three short sittings: the first at 00:36 lands plates four, five, three and six in that order, the second at 00:45 to 00:47 lands the three tall night crops, and the last puts plates one and two down at 04:31:36, two seconds after the final two Airport City frames landed. The gallery names its subject but no place, and the frames name nothing legible beyond a road, street lighting and palms; several of the files carry a machine offset of -07:00 rather than Accra’s +00:00, so the timestamps describe a computer, not a site. Outside the source, a public encyclopaedia entry for a Villagio Vista in Accra describes three towers colour-coded yellow, red and green and completed in 2016, which matches the three cladding colours visible here and the open unglazed storeys in the night frames, and is offered only as corroboration of what these pictures show rather than as evidence for anything the listing claims. The source is on Behance — VILLAGIO VISTA - PHOTOGRAPHY EXPERIMENT, 9 July 2015.
