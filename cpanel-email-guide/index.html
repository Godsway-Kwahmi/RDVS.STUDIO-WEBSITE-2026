So the first fix is DNS, not a toggle. The rest is authentication. Do the steps in order; step 1 is a read-only check that protects the website.

Before you start - how to reach cPanel cpanel.rdvs.studio and webmail.rdvs.studio do not resolve today because their subdomain records do not exist. Log in through Namecheap: Dashboard → Hosting List → Manage next to rdvs.studio → Go to cPanel. Once step 6 is done, cpanel.rdvs.studio will work as well.

1Open Zone Editor and confirm the zone exists

In cPanel, open Domains → Zone Editor. The landing page lists every domain the hosting account controls.

Before changing anything, confirm these three records. Click Manage to see the table.

TypeNameValue
A@ (the bare domain)192.64.117.211
CNAMEwwwrdvs.studio
MX@priority 10, host mail.rdvs.studio - or the three Jellyfish hosts (see the note below)
If the MX row reads Jellyfish, leave it alone A cPanel zone on Namecheap shared hosting often shows three MX rows - preferences 5, 10 and 20, hosts mx1-hosting.jellyfish.systems, mx2-hosting.jellyfish.systems and mx3-hosting.jellyfish.systems - instead of one row pointing at mail.rdvs.studio. That is Namecheap's spam filter: inbound mail passes through Jellyfish and is then handed to the cPanel mailbox. It is a correct configuration, not a fault. Replacing it with mail.rdvs.studio bypasses the filter.
Stop condition If rdvs.studio is not listed at all, the domain is not attached to this cPanel account. Do not change nameservers until that is fixed, or the website loses its DNS. Add the domain under Domains in cPanel or contact Namecheap support first.

The record table has a type filter above it - useful when you need to find SPF, DKIM and DMARC TXT records among hundreds of rows.

2Add the missing mail A record

This is the record whose absence makes mail.rdvs.studio nonexistent, which in turn makes the MX unusable.

Click + A Record on the rdvs.studio row (or Manage → + Add Record → A), then enter:

TypeA
Namemail
Address192.64.117.211
TTL14400 (or Automatic)

Save, then filter by mail and confirm you now have an A record pointing at 192.64.117.211. Nothing else in the zone changes.

3Enable DKIM

Go to Email → Email Deliverability. The list shows every domain and a status. Click Manage next to rdvs.studio.

On the Manage screen you will see a DKIM block. If it says a record does not exist, cPanel shows the exact record it wants:

Two ways to install it - pick one A. Click Install the suggested record right here. It works only when the domain already uses Namecheap's hosting nameservers, which is why it may be unavailable before step 6.
B. Copy the Name and Value, then add them in Zone Editor → + Add Record → TXT yourself. This works regardless of nameservers.

Enabling DKIM in cPanel usually writes the TXT into the local zone automatically as well. After clicking enable (or installing it), filter Zone Editor by TXT and confirm default._domainkey is present. If it is not, use method B.

4Add DMARC as a TXT record

cPanel's deliverability screen checks DMARC, but on Namecheap you create the record in Zone Editor. Namecheap's own guide says the same: "log in to cPanel → Zone Editor and create the corresponding record as a TXT one."

TypeTXT
Name_dmarc
Valuev=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@rdvs.studio; pct=100
TTL14400 (or Automatic)

cPanel appends the zone to the name, so the published record is _dmarc.rdvs.studio. Keep it as a single TXT; do not wrap it or split it across two records. A shorter value of just v=DMARC1; p=none; is also valid - it simply asks for no reports. Add the rua tag later, once the mailbox exists.

Create the reporting mailbox first rua=mailto:dmarc@rdvs.studio points at a mailbox that must exist, otherwise the reports bounce. Create dmarc@rdvs.studio under Email → Email Accounts, or omit the rua tag for now and add it later. Reports will not arrive until step 2 is live anyway.

Start at p=none. That asks receivers to monitor and report, not to block. Move to p=quarantine only after two to four weeks of clean reports - and do that deliberately, because a hard policy applied before alignment is proven can reject your own legitimate mail.

5Confirm there is exactly one SPF record

Filter the zone by TXT. SPF is the record whose value starts v=spf1. There must be exactly one.

Current live valuev=spf1 +a +mx +ip4:192.64.117.211 include:spf.web-hosting.com ~all

That record already authorises the correct server IP. If cPanel's installer adds another SPF while you are in here, delete the older one - two SPF records combine into a permanent error and break authentication.

Optional tidy-up: v=spf1 ip4:192.64.117.211 include:spf.web-hosting.com ~all does the same job without +a and +mx. Dropping +mx removes a mechanism that reads the MX target - the very record that was broken. Not required, but one less failure mode.

If your panel offers a Customize button on SPF, it edits the same record; the preview it shows before saving is the thing to read. See the two states below, an SPF being repaired and the confirmation afterwards.

6Switch the domain to Namecheap Web Hosting DNS

Now that the zone contains the mail host, DKIM and DMARC, hand authority to it. In Namecheap: Domain List → rdvs.studio → Nameservers → Namecheap Web Hosting DNS.

The nameservers are dns1.namecheaphosting.com and dns2.namecheaphosting.com. The old BasicDNS zone keeps answering while resolvers move, so there is no dead interval; mail is already broken, so propagation cannot make it worse.

When the switch is visible, return to Email Deliverability and use Repair on anything still flagged. That one-click path is only available when the domain is on these nameservers - the reason for doing this step before giving up on a greyed-out Install button.

From this point on, cPanel is the live DNS Namecheap's Advanced DNS page becomes a stored copy that the internet ignores. Add, edit and delete records in Zone Editor only. Editing the wrong one is the most common silent failure after a nameserver switch.

Verify before moving on:

nslookup -type=NS rdvs.studio 8.8.8.8
nslookup mail.rdvs.studio 8.8.8.8
nslookup -type=TXT default._domainkey.rdvs.studio 8.8.8.8
nslookup -type=TXT _dmarc.rdvs.studio 8.8.8.8

Expect the two hosting nameservers, then 192.64.117.211, then two TXT answers. MX caches are one hour, so allow an hour before concluding that mail routing did not move.

If the delegation changed but the records did not This is a real state: the .studio registry already lists dns1/dns2.namecheaphosting.com, yet querying those nameservers still returns the old BasicDNS zone (NS registrar-servers.com, MX mail.rdvs.studio, no mail A). It means Namecheap has not activated the cPanel zone yet. Allow up to 24 hours. If it persists, check that the Nameservers field in the dashboard holds the Namecheap Web Hosting DNS preset and not Custom DNS with those two hostnames, then ask support to activate the hosting zone for the domain.

7Run AutoSSL

Go to Security → SSL/TLS Certificates and open the Status tab. On older cPanel builds this page is called SSL/TLS Status. It lists the domain and www with a lock or a warning.

If a name is unsecured, run AutoSSL / resolve the listed issue. AutoSSL also runs on its own schedule, but running it now avoids waiting for the next pass. The domain has no CAA record, so nothing restricts Let's Encrypt issuance. Do not touch Keys or paste a private key to fix this - the certificate is managed for you.

8Prove that mail actually works

DNS records being present is not the same as mail being delivered. Test both directions.

Outbound

From the mailbox (webmail or a mail client), send to a Gmail address you control. In Gmail open the message → Show original. Expect SPF: PASS, DKIM: PASS with domain rdvs.studio, and DMARC: PASS. If DKIM shows a different domain or none, the record is not the one being used to sign - recheck step 3.

Then run one real message through mail-tester.com and aim for 9/10 or better, with all three authentication lines green.

Inbound

Send from Gmail to the @rdvs.studio mailbox and confirm it arrives. This is the test that was impossible before step 2; if it fails, the MX or its A record is still wrong.

9If something still fails

SymptomWhere to look
Mail bounces with "host not found"Zone Editor: the mail A record is missing or the MX points at a different name. Re-run the four lookups in step 6.
Message deferred or rejected with a numberEmail → Track Delivery: filter by recipient and read the delivery event. Mail Queue shows retries.
Inbound lands in JunkEmail → Spam Filters: check the score, whitelist the sender. FormSubmit submissions come from formsubmit.co.
Mail forwarded to Gmail is marked spamForwarding breaks SPF alignment at the destination. Fetch by IMAP instead of forwarding while diagnosing.
Gmail says "unauthenticated"DKIM missing for the exact selector in use, or the From domain does not align with SPF. Compare the DKIM d= value in Show original with default._domainkey.
"Domain has exceeded the max emails per hour"Namecheap/WHM hourly limit. Wait for the window to reset; this is a rate limit, not a spam verdict.
PTR warning in Email DeliverabilityExpected on a shared IP (server306-1.web-hosting.com). It cannot be changed here and is not what blocks Gmail once SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass.
