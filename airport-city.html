Photography / Architectural Photography — 2015 · RDVS Studios

Airport City

Airport City is a 2015 in-house photography series in which RDVS treated Accra’s new business district as a set of framing problems: a blue curtain wall used as a mirror, a skyline crushed into a strip only 287 pixels tall, and two vertical frames shot downward through the gap between concrete floor slabs.

The gallery is titled AIRPORT CITY,ACCRA - PHOTOGRAPHY EXPERIMENT and was published on 5 July 2015 from the studio’s RDVS. DESIGN Behance account, under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike badge. Its description is a statement about the category rather than about these pictures: “This project falls into our in house category of ‘Experimental photography’. In our quest and search for the perfect shot we embark on these experiments that allow us to bend (and sometimes break) the rules of photography. In some cases we are successful at other times this excercise becomes a wild goose choose but we love these experiments because we are able to see things from a different angle/view than we usually do.” The two misspellings are the studio’s own and are left exactly as written. Five frames were published and all five are plated here.

The credits run in full: “TEAM SHOTS TAKEN + CURATED BY: JUDE NYOAGBE + RANDY BINEY POST PRODUCTION BY RANDY BINEY.” Both names recur through the archive as the designer, renderer or animator on client work rather than as crew brought in for a shoot, and the split inside the line is the informative part — taking the pictures and choosing between them were the same two people, while the finishing fell to one of them. Every frame carries the same red RDVSIMAGING badge in its lower-right corner, so the series went out under the studio’s imaging tag rather than under RDVS itself.

The first three frames are landscape and they play one idea three ways. The curtain wall in plate one is not photographed as a surface but used as a mirror: its lower panes give back a neighbouring facade of black, white and grey checkerboard with the sign from that building’s roof reading backwards in the glass, so the district appears twice in one exposure and neither appearance is direct. Plate two takes the ground away altogether, cropping two parapets — a red Vodafone wordmark on white banding, a gold and black monogram roundel on blue glass — against a sky burned to flat white. Plate three takes the opposite extreme and crushes the whole skyline into a strip 800 by 287 pixels, so shallow that the buildings are reduced to rooflines, signage and a crane jib crossing in front of them.

Plates four and five are the two vertical frames, and they are where the rules actually bend. Both look down from an upper floor through the gap between concrete slabs, and the slabs stay in the picture: a dark floor soffit across the top and a pale parapet across the bottom cut the view into an irregular aperture, so a car park, a mall’s cylindrical corner tower and a checkered facade beyond are all seen as though through a slot in the building itself. They are also the only two frames wider than 800 pixels, at 1000 by 1376 and 1000 by 1583, and plate five returns to the same checkerboard cladding that plate one catches only in reflection — this time as a direct subject, with its rooftop sign photographed from behind so that its letters run the other way.

The listing takes 2015 from the gallery, but the two dates the source carries disagree. Behance stamps the project as published on 5 July 2015, while the five image files carry upload stamps inside one six-and-a-half-hour session spanning 1 and 2 November 2015 — the three landscape frames first, the two vertical ones about six hours later. That is a single sitting rather than the “ever evolving project” the description promises, and nothing has been added since. The year reads 2015 on either date, so the disagreement moves nothing on this page.

Airport City was not a one-off either. The studio’s Behance list puts LA MAISON - PHOTOGRAPHY on 11 June 2015 and WEST HILLS MALL - PHOTOGRAPHY on 14 June, then this gallery on 5 July, VILLAGIO VISTA - PHOTOGRAPHY EXPERIMENT on 9 July and LINES - EXPERIMENTAL PHOTOGRAPHY on 26 July: five photography galleries in six weeks, none of them in this archive yet.
